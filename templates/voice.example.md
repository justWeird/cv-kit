# Writing Voice — Template

Copy this to `profile/voice.md` and fill it in, or let `/cv-setup` derive it from your writing
samples and then edit what it produced.

Every `<...>` marker is a prompt for you, not text to keep. Delete the guidance paragraphs once
you have written your answers. The finished file should read as a set of rules, not as a form.

Two skills read this file. `/jd` reads it in full before writing a cover letter, and applies
the persona. `/proposal` does the same. The tailored CV uses only the Hard Constraints section,
never the persona, because CV bullets follow a stricter and plainer register.

If you want a working file with no persona at all, use `voice.neutral.md` instead.

---

## Voice

<Who are you talking to when you write? An equal, a colleague, a stranger you respect? Write
two or three sentences.>

<What stance do you take toward your own work? Understated, direct, enthusiastic? Give the rule,
not the adjective. For example: "State the result and let it carry the claim. Do not attach an
adjective to yourself.">

<What do you do with a weakness or a gap? Name it early, work around it, leave it out? Be
explicit, because this is the single decision that most changes how a cover letter reads.>

## Hard Constraints

This section is the one the CV uses. Keep it mechanical and checkable.

### Never use

<List every word and phrase you never want to see in your own writing. Be specific. A list of
20 banned phrases is more useful than a paragraph about avoiding clichés.>

<Start from this set if you have no list of your own:>

- Em dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons, or parentheses.
- "Delve into", "nuanced", "tapestry", "straightforward"
- "It's worth noting that", "It is important to note", "In conclusion", "To summarize"
- Any sentence that starts with "In today's world"
- Passive voice used to avoid ownership

### Punctuation rules

<Em dashes: yes or no. Parentheses: for what. Colons: for what. Serial comma: yes or no.>

### Formatting rules

<Bullets in a cover letter: yes or no. Bold inside prose: yes or no. Headers in short-form
writing: yes or no.>
